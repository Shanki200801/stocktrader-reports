How to use this report

Daily briefArchive
This is paper-trading research, not investment advice. No money is traded, and nothing here promises any return.

What each page shows

  • Daily brief: the paper book (₹1,00,000 start) and shadow books, NAV versus the Nifty 500, holdings, recent orders, live prediction tracker, positions history, data checks and news on held names.
  • Screen: the formula-ranked list of Nifty 500 stocks per momentum method, with every input shown.
  • Archive: every past report date with links.
  • Evidence: backtests of each method on past data, after Indian trading costs.

Statuses

  • CANDIDATE: passed part of the backtest gates; being watched live. Not proven.
  • REJECTED: failed the backtest gates; shown for transparency.
  • INSUFFICIENT_DATA: too little history to judge.
  • DEMOTED: live results stayed below the backtest band for 3 consecutive monthly reviews.
  • HALTED: the book's strategy was demoted and the book moved to cash. Choosing a replacement is a human decision.

How to read the screen

  • NMS (momentum score): higher means stronger risk-adjusted 6- and 12-month momentum compared with the rest of the universe.
  • trend_ok: the price is above its 200-day average and the 50-day is above the 200-day. Failing names are dropped by the "+T" variants.
  • Regime: ON when the Nifty 500 is above its 200-day average. When OFF, "+T" variants hold cash.

How the paper book decides

  • Once a month (month-end) rank the eligible universe and target the top 8, equal weight.
  • Hold buffer: a stock already held is kept while its rank is 16 or better, which cuts churn.
  • Whole shares only; orders fill at the next trading day's open; full Indian costs (brokerage-free delivery taxes and charges, exchange fees, slippage) are charged.

How to judge whether it is working

  • Look at the matured table: hit rate (share of picks that beat the equal-weight universe) and mean excess, against the backtest expectation beside it. Results inside the backtest band are normal; persistent results below it lead to demotion.
  • The interim tracker for open predictions is noise until the horizon (63 or 126 trading days) is reached. Do not act on it.
  • Small numbers of matured predictions prove little; 12 or more monthly signal dates are needed before any promotion.

Risks

  • The backtest maximum drawdown was about -67%. Momentum can fall sharply and for long periods, and it can crash when markets reverse.
  • Backtests have limits: survivorship in the universe, data errors, costs and liquidity assumptions, and the fact that past patterns can stop working.
  • On small accounts, fixed charges and slippage take a larger share of returns; whole-share rounding leaves some cash idle.

If you use this to inform real decisions

  • It is paper research, not advice, and makes no promise of returns.
  • Wait for live evidence first: at least 6 to 12 months of matured predictions before trusting it.
  • If you follow it at all, follow the rules systematically rather than cherry-picking names you like.
  • Size positions so a large loss is tolerable; never risk more than you can afford to lose.
  • Respect the news review flags on holdings before acting.
  • Costs and taxes matter (as commonly stated: short-term gains 20%, long-term gains 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh; verify current rules).
  • For personal decisions, consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
